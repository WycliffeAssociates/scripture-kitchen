## 1. Placement

Placement is the engine's main rule. Every example in this pass is a
Placement row, and every one reduces to the same two faults.

### The examples

```text
GEN 14:2   …(also called Zoar).        . after punctuation    74/38,776   26 books
GEN 47:9   …sojourning are 130. The…   . after a digit       116/38,776    9 books
EXO 38:26  …older—603,550 men          — before a digit        2/1,795     2 books
(en_ulb)   …Kadesh), and defeated      , after punctuation   103/54,723   30 books
```

### Fault 1: too sensitive, because the judgment is one-sided

The row asks only "of all periods, how many follow a digit". At 38,776
periods the band's cutoff is about 0.3%, so any secondary convention under
that fires, however steady it is. The other side asks "of all numbers, how
many end in a period", and there `130.` is ordinary.

**Rule: a Placement row fires only if the pairing is unusual from both
sides**, for the glyph and for the class it touches.

| Class it touches | The other side |
|---|---|
| Punctuation (`Nonletter`) | ExactNeighbor, judged by whichever member leads the pair |
| Digit | Number ends (or starts): digit-pool occurrences whose next (or prev) is not a digit |
| Letter, Space | Word ends and starts touching a mark; the fleet shows this never clears a row (below) |

For punctuation this is the deferral. An occurrence leaves Placement's
numerator when the leader's ExactNeighbor is **entitled**, whether or not that
row fired, because a fired row already reports the pair. It stays in the
denominator, since it is still an occurrence. An unentitled leader leaves it
to Placement, so abstention still falls back.

Fleet results under the default config:

| Class | Rows | Occurrences | After the rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nonletter | 81 | 1,120 | 140 remain; 39 rows silent |
| Digit | 30 | 280 | 7 rows silent (en `.` after a digit 116, and small French rows) |
| Letter | 47 | 274 | none silent |
| Space | 30 | 121 | none silent |

- The comma row after punctuation, per corpus: en 103 → 0, nya 110 → 1,
  swh 146 → 2, spa 38 → 2, fra 124 → 5, hin 53 → 0. The explained leaders are
  `)` everywhere, French `»` and `]`, and Hindi `’`. The rule never names a
  glyph.
- Letter and Space rows never clear, and they read as real: `?` followed
  directly by a letter (a missing space) and similar. The both-sides check
  adds nothing there and costs nothing.
- In en_ulb the real tail still fires, through ExactNeighbor:

  ```text
  1SA   …with sticks?," and the Philistine     ? then ,   1/1073
  2KI   …said, "Shoot!", and he shot           " then ,   1/129
  JOS   …'We are very far from you', when      ' then ,   4/496
  JER   …'The burden of Yahweh', this is       (same row)
  ```

- Not every `Nonletter` neighbour is a pair inside a mark run. A digit is not
  a run atom, so `(1` gives the digit a `Nonletter` neighbour that
  ExactNeighbor never judged. Those stay with Placement.
- **Harness caveat.** A few rows (French `«` 17, Spanish `¿` 17) have
  `Nonletter` neighbours that sit in no run. The vref harness joins chapters
  with no separator, so a chapter's first glyph touches the last one of the
  previous chapter. Real USFM projection ends lines with `\n`. The same
  artifact is in `pattern_volume`'s fleet numbers, so this needs checking
  before either is treated as fact.
- This narrows `judge.md`'s "channels are independent" to: an entitled finer
  judgment is not counted twice.
- To verify at build time: the Expediter's chapter cache still holds, since
  the rule is decided at judge time, which is corpus-level. Galley's
  equivalence tests cover it.

### Fault 2: too blind to a habit concentrated in one book

```text
nya, comma after a space:  1,417 of 46,382 commas = 3.1%   → no row at all
  1SA            1,183 / 1,526   78%
  rest of nya      234 / 44,856   0.5%   (42 other books, 2 or fewer percent each)
```

Pooled, the share is too common to fire, and counting books is no help,
because 42 books of stray cases look widespread. What separates 1SA is its
rate.

**Rule: a book fires when its rate breaks from the median rate of the rest.**

- Judged books: those where the glyph itself occurs at least the support
  floor. Jude without `)` is not judged for `)`.
- Baseline: the **median** of the other judged books' rates, with the book
  under test left out, so a dominant book cannot pull the baseline toward
  itself. This matters most in a 3-book project.
- Fires when the book's rate is at least N× the baseline and above a minimum
  rate (config ratios, strict if wanted early in a project). MAD is at most
  an option, since it degenerates at 3 books.
- Language-neutral, and a separate rule from Placement's band.

### The census: held

Book-by-book counts per glyph, side and class are the evidence for Fault 2
and for showing "1SA 78%, the rest 0.5%". Held for now. Measure its size
before deciding whether it goes on the wire or runs as a cold, unjudged loop.

### What's normal

The usual class and its share ("usually a letter, 99.7%"). It is free:
`PlacementEvidence` (`judge/scalars.rs:409`) already holds all five class
counts for both sides. On the wire: a u8 class and a u16 share in the pattern
row, which grows 24 → 28 bytes (about +0.5 KB on en_ulb). It goes in the
version 2 bump.

### The breakdown: decided after the rule lands

Only a punctuation-class row can have one: its neighbours come from the
ExactNeighbor counts. A letter, space or digit neighbour's exact character is
never recorded. The fault-1 rule silences most punctuation rows, and what
remains is mostly a handful of sites whose text already shows the neighbour.

If it ships, it is a discriminated union the format enforces:

- A new variable-length section, `(pattern index, neighbour scalar, count)` at
  12 bytes per row.
- A letter-class row that carries a breakdown is a decode error, the way
  `decode_pattern` already refuses rows the encoder cannot have written.

Decide it from the fleet's remaining punctuation rows after fault 1.

## 2. RunShape

RunShape asks how big the cluster of marks around a glyph is, and whether it
is all one mark: "`;` usually stands alone, and here it sits in a cluster of
2 different marks". Placement asks what class touches one side, and
ExactNeighbor asks which exact mark follows.

### The examples

```text
GEN 20:13  …"He is my brother."'"   " in a cluster of 4   31/12,015  14 books
1KI 4:13   …gate bars);             ; in a cluster of 2   26/4,904   10 books
ISA 49:9   …'Come out;'             (same row)
```

What is inside each row:

```text
" cluster of 4    ."'"×27  ?"'"×2  .'?"×1  "...×1
; cluster of 2    );×8  ';×7  ";×6  ;'×3  ;"×2
```

- `."'"` closes a triple-nested quote, the house convention (27 in 14 books).
- `);` is ordinary.
- `;'` against `';` is an order swap: the semicolon inside the quote 3 times,
  outside it 7 times. That is the real signal, buried under 23 ordinary
  clusters.
- `.'?"` is almost certainly a mistake.

### Missing → kitchen changes

**Rule: the same one-sided fault; the other side is whether the exact cluster
recurs.** A cluster whose exact sequence occurs at least `support_floor`
times in the corpus is a convention and leaves the row's numerator. It stays
in the denominator. The exact clusters and their counts already exist
(`BookAggregate::runs()`).

- **Absolute count, reusing `support_floor` (5).** No new setting. The question
  is whether there is enough evidence to call it a habit, which is what
  `support_floor` already means. A share of the cluster's own shape loses the
  swap: `;'` is 3 of 26, which is 11% and reads as normal. Split it into its
  own setting only if the fleet shows it is too coarse.
- **Exact sequences, not pooled.** Grouping by kind of mark (quote,
  terminal…) folds `;'` and `;"` into "semicolon then quote" (5) and clears the
  swap.
- The price: a correct but rare variant still fires (`?"'"`×2). The message
  is what makes it cheap to dismiss.
- It behaves strictly early in a project, where there are few repeats. A wrong
  cluster repeated 5 or more times in a large project passes. If those repeats
  are concentrated in a book, the fault-2 rule catches it.

Fleet results under the default config:

| Variant | Rows | Clusters left | Rows silent | en `"`/4, `;`/2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 163 | 1,111 | 0 | 31, 26 |
| Exact cluster recurs ≥ 5 | 163 | 448 | 10 | 4, 5 |
| Pooled by kind of mark | 163 | 399 | 12 | 2, 0 |

**Which clusters.** A RunShape row is explainable only with its clusters. The
engine holds them exactly, so this breakdown is the one worth shipping for
this row. It is what shows `';`×7 beside `;'`×3.

### Out of scope: quote balance

RunShape does not track nesting. Nesting shows up as recurring closing
clusters, which recurrence handles. Balance, meaning a quote opened and never
closed, is a separate candidate rule, and a weak one for straight quotes:
ASCII `"` and `'` look the same whether they open or close, `'` is also the
apostrophe, and direction could only be guessed from spacing. It is workable
for curly quotes. Not planned.

## 3. Doubled

### The example

```text
GEN 1:2 (edited)  …moving above the surface surface of the the waters.
                  the the          → fires     1 of 54,687 "the"
                  surface surface  → silent    1 of ~47 "surface"
```

`surface` is not being excused as a particle. The per-word band silences it:
one doubling is 2.1% of its uses, and for a word used 21–100 times the band
fires only under 1%. So a word used fewer than about 100 times can never fire
on a single doubling, and under `word_support_floor` (20) it is not judged at
all. That is most of the vocabulary, which is where typos land.

### Missing → kitchen changes

**Rule: guilty until innocent.** Every doubling fires, unless:

- **This word doubles habitually**: it is doubled at least `support_floor`
  (5) times in the corpus. An absolute count, for the same reason as RunShape.
  `vous vous` ×148 passes.
- **The corpus doubles productively**: the existing recusal
  (`doubles_productive_bp`, 3% of distinct words doubled twice or more)
  switches the channel off corpus-wide.
- **The host turned it off**: the existing `DoublesPolicy::Never`.

The per-word band and `word_support_floor` no longer apply to this channel.

**Reduplicating languages: keep both, and nothing cleverer.** A rough regex
count (not an engine run) of bare doublings that fire:

| Corpus | Now | Guilty until innocent |
|---|---|---|
| en | ~3 | ~10 |
| fra | 4 | 10 |
| swh | 35 | ~78 |
| nya | 44 | ~165 |

The Bantu jump is mostly genuine reduplication (`bwino bwino`, `manje
manje`, 1–4 times each). Keep the automatic recusal and the manual off
switch. A project where it floods turns the channel off. No per-language
detection beyond the existing 3% share. The fleet sweep checks where the
recusal lands once every doubling counts.

**Separators: two settings, spaces only and any punctuation.**

| Between the two words | Setting | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Spaces only (bare) | on | `the the`, `surface surface` |
| Any punctuation (separated) | on, its own switch | JDG 5:3 `I, I will sing`, `Moses, Moses` |
| A verse boundary | never | JOB 20:7–8 `…'Where is he?'` then `\v 8 He will fly…` |

- Never across a verse boundary is new. Today the separated lane lets line
  breaks and verse boundaries through, so JOB 20:7–8 is a row.
- With "any punctuation" as a single switch, a quote that opens speech still
  counts (`this, 'This`). That is accepted: one switch is simpler than a
  policy per kind of mark, and the separator's last mark is in the data if
  that ever has to change.
- No case setting. The separator decides it. A bare `The the` is a real
  typo. A capital after a quote or a sentence end sits behind punctuation,
  which the separated switch governs.

## 5. Casing

### The example

```text
GEN 30:28  Then he said, "Name your wages, and I will pay them."
row        Title "Name" 2/883 free positions
```

A false positive, from a deliberate design choice that is wrong for English.
The terminal table learns which glyphs force a capital (`.` 99.9% does, `,`
10% does not) and treats quotes as transparent. So `said, "Name` records the
comma, which does not force, and `Name` counts as a free capital. But in this
project `, "` is almost always followed by a capital, because speech starts
with one. The comma's 10% is diluted by every comma that is not in front of
speech.

This appears to be most of the en_ulb Title rows. All 3 sites of the `Was`
row are `, "Was` (`said to Gehazi, "Was…`, `says, "Was…`, `to another,
"Was…`).

### Missing → kitchen changes

**Rule: learn "mark then opening quote" as its own context**, beside the bare
mark, in the terminal table, and let the counts decide:

```text
,      → capital 10%    → does not force
, "    → capital ~99%   → forces, so "Name" is not judged
```

- No list and no config. It covers `«`, `„` and the rest in any language,
  since the rule never names a mark.
- It stays honest where a project writes speech in lowercase after a comma:
  `, "` does not reach `terminal_upper_share_bp` there, and a capital is
  still judged.
- Cost: the follows lane has to record that an opening quote intervened.
  That touches the chapter row, so it gets the usual cache and equivalence
  checks.
- A user list of marks to ignore after is a possible manual override on top,
  like `DoublesPolicy`. Not built first.
- **What's normal is missing here too.** The row carries this form's count
  and the word's free positions, but not which form is usual or its count.

## 6. SentenceStart, Rarity, LetterRun

No rule change for any of the three. Today's wording hides the numbers the
rows already carry.

```text
JOB 41:14-15  …which are a terror? / his back is…   ? then lowercase: 3 of 2,163 cased handoffs
JOS 21:5      Kohath’s                              ’ 2 in the whole project, 2 books
PSA 81:1      joyfullly                             "l" ×3: 1 of 24,318 repeats of "l"
```

- **JOB 41:15** is almost certainly a real miss ("His").
- **JOS 21:5**: the project writes the apostrophe `'` everywhere else (about

- **PSA 81:1**: LetterRun's denominator is that letter's own repeats, so the

### Missing → kitchen changes

- **Rarity: the usual mark of the same kind.** `’` and `'` share a pool
  (Quote), so "the most common mark in the same pool" is cheap at judge time.
  It turns the finding into "the project writes `'` instead". It is not
  derivable by Sefer, since scalar counts are not on the wire. Version 2.

# Messaging

This tool does not work unless a reviewer understands the why behind every
squiggle, as plainly as possible. Messaging is its own contract: kitchen
supplies the why as data and reference wording, and Sefer renders and
localizes it.

## What every squiggle must answer

1. **What was seen.** The glyph or word, and what it touches.
2. **How often.** The count against the total it is measured against, named
   in the glyph's own terms: "of 54,723 commas", never "of sites".
3. **What is normal instead.** The usual alternative and its share.
4. **How widespread.** Books with it, of books in the project.

A finding that cannot answer 3 is not explainable yet. That is a kitchen gap,
not a wording problem.

## Rules

- **Say it from the glyph's point of view, and say "touching".** "Comes
  before a digit" reads as location (anywhere earlier in the sentence). The
  claim is adjacency: "“—” comes right before a digit, with nothing between".
- **`PlacementBefore` reads backwards in English.** It means the neighbour is
  before, so the glyph comes *after* it. GEN 47:9 is labelled "Before" and
  reads "`.` comes right after a digit". Descriptor ids name the glyph's
  relation: `placement.follows` (neighbour before) and `placement.precedes`
  (neighbour after).
- **Silence is not "all clear".** The tool finds a minority against a
  majority. With no majority there is nothing to measure against, so no
  findings means no convention was found, not that the text is consistent.
  Sefer must never render an empty list as a pass.
- **A genuine minority still needs the why.** EXO 38:26 is correct, unusual
  style: 2 of 1,795 em dashes come right before a digit, and a letter usually
  follows one. It is unusual from both sides, so it stays. Its value is a
  message plain enough to dismiss in a second: the usual pattern, plus the
  row's sites, which are every em dash touching a digit.
- **One headline per squiggle.** "RunShape, PlacementBefore" is one site
  matching two rows. The site is listed under its finest row, and the others
  have zero sites of their own. Show that headline's sentence only. The
  fault-1 rule removes most of these doubles anyway.
- **Show the cluster and what the project does instead.** "`;'`: this project
  usually writes `';` (7 times). This order appears 3 times." For a
  RunShape row, "cluster of 2 different marks" replaces "run shape mixed
  len 2".
- **Counts, not percents, for a doubling.** Today's "0.11% of sites" is the
  doubling's share of the word's own uses (1 of 895 "Moses"). Say it as
  counts: "“Moses” is written twice in a row here (“Moses, Moses”). The
  project does this nowhere else; “Moses” appears 895 times." When a doubling
  is excused as a habit, say that too ("done 148 times in this project"), so
  a silence can be explained.
- **Name the pair, what usually follows, and a swap.** NUM 7:69: "Two
  periods in a row (`..`). This happens 7 times in 5,085." GEN 48:20: "Period
  outside the quote (`'.`). This project puts it inside 975 times." DEU 7:17:
  "`'` followed directly by `—`. Usually `'` is followed by `"` (416 of 496);
  this pairing appears 6 times in 5 books."
- **Say what the case is and what it usually is.** Not "this word is used
  unconventionally", but "“Name” is capitalized here. In mid-sentence it is
  otherwise written “name”." For all capitals: "written in all capitals here
  (“GOD”); elsewhere “God”." The usual form waits on kitchen. Until then:
  "2 of its 883 mid-sentence uses are capitalized."
- **SentenceStart names the mark's habit**: "After “?”, this project
  capitalizes the next word 2,160 of 2,163 times. Here “his” is lowercase."
- **Rarity gives the total**: "“’” appears only 2 times in this whole
  project." Then, once kitchen ships it: "…the project writes “'”
  elsewhere."
- **LetterRun stays letter-specific**: "“l” is written three times in a row
  here (“lll”). Of the 24,318 places this project repeats “l”, this is the
  only one with three." Never "no letter repeats three times": the row
  cannot back that.

## Clunky → kitchen reworks or ships a helper

- Ids are engine vocabulary: `Nonletter` defines by absence, the side is
  `prev`/`next` in the key but `PlacementBefore` in the reasons, and the
  channel repeats inside the reasons (Sefer dedupes it by hand).
- The band is a staircase step number, not the cutoff it stands for.
- **Descriptor helper in `sous-chef/reader.ts`**, returning data rather than
  language:

  ```text
  { id: "convention.placement.follows",
    params: { glyph: ",", neighbor: "punctuation", count: 103, total: 54723,
              books: 30, bookTotal: 66, usual: "letter", usualShare: 9970 } }
  ```

  One id per relation, so a translator always sees a whole sentence. The
  class is a `select` enum, and numbers stay raw for the consumer's `Intl`.
- **English reference catalog** in ICU MessageFormat beside the helper, e.g.
  `{count} of {total} “{glyph}” come right after {neighbor, select,
  punctuation {a punctuation mark} letter {a letter} space {a space} digit
  {a digit}}`. Sefer copies it, owns the final strings and adds languages.
  Kitchen ships no translation runtime.
- **Guide**: the four questions, the rules above, what each id means, which
  parameter answers which question, and what never to say ("error",
  "wrong").
