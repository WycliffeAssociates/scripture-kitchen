## What a user gets

A translator's project is checked for consistent rendering of its key terms, in words the
translator already reads: "Where your source says *grace*, you usually write *neema* (236 of
241 verses); JHN 9:38 has none of them." Behind that sentence the term is a Greek or Hebrew
lemma and sense, so *grace*, *graces* and *gracious* are one term, and *grace* the gift and
*thanks* are two. The user never has to see Greek.

Two paths, chosen by what the host can ship:

- **Pivot (the default).** MACULA lemmas and senses as the key, both testaments, and the
  gateway text (usually ULB, in English, Spanish or French) for every label. The labels come
  from a precomputed gateway↔MACULA alignment, so the host passes in nothing but the project.
- **Gateway only** (if MACULA can't ship). The gateway text is the source itself, checked by
  word. It's weaker: *believe*, *believes* and *believed* are three terms (see the gateway-only
  section).

## Why no new engine is needed

`pairing` already takes opaque units. The crate doc says "Units are opaque to this crate: a
key, source lemma ids and target text", and each `SourceWord` is a `u32` id plus a `content`
flag. Every check judges only `content` words (`findings.rs`: the rendering counts at line
105, the checkable set at line 173, coverage at line 287). A pivot, a gateway-only source and
a per-sense key are all just different ids and flags in the same `UnitInput`.

> 3 way align and just trust whatever the greek en_ulb side says

Yes, on fragmentation. Aligned to English words, πιστεύω's verses split across *believe*,
*believes*, *believed* and *believing*. Each is a smaller population, fewer reach the
checkable floor, and the habit looks weaker than it is. Lemmas avoid that, with no
language-specific stemmer.

The three-way alignment works, and most of it can happen offline:

```text
offline, once per gateway text      en_ulb ↔ MACULA       Model 2 on lemmas, both testaments
                                    → gateway pack: per MACULA word, the ULB word(s) it links to
on the device, per project          target ↔ MACULA       Model 2 on lemmas (today's engine)
a label, at read time               lemma·sense in MRK 2:17 → ULB words linked to it in MRK 2:17
                                    → "where ULB says *grace*"; across all verses: its usual ULB words
```

- **Labels come from the gateway's own words, per verse.** "Your source says *grace* here"
  is literally true: it's the ULB word linked to that Greek word in that verse. The term's
  label is its most common ULB word, so χάρις·88.66 reads *grace* and χάρις·33.350 reads
  *thanks*.
- **The gloss is the fallback** when the gateway link is weak (a paraphrase, an untranslated
  particle). It comes from the UBS dictionaries in the UI language, or MACULA's English gloss.
- **The gateway alignment is computed once per gateway text, not per project, and can run on
  the device.** It's the same engine (Model 2 on lemmas), so any text the host passes in can be
  a gateway. It runs in a worker as a cold, background job outside the edit loop, and the
  result is cached on the device, keyed by the text's checksum. A prebuilt pack from R2 is only
  a shortcut to the same bytes. Deterministic output makes "fetched" and "computed" provably
  identical, and a test can hold them to it.
- **It is "trust what the Greek↔ULB side says"**, so its errors become label errors, never
  findings. The check runs on target↔MACULA alone, and the gateway only names things.

## Polysemy: train per lemma, judge per sense, merging is never an error

Will:

> words are polysemic and therefore you should align to a sense of semantic and not a word
> across the entire corpus? […] But then again, Hebrew grace may not be the exact same as
> Greek grace.

> a language that retains those two senses into one may not be wrong. They may just use
> context to split out those senses. But it's also worth potentially flagging to them there
> are two senses of a word here.

```text
train    key = lemma              t(target | χάρις) is a mixture; links are per occurrence
check    key = (lemma, sense)     χάρις·88.66 (89 tokens) checkable alone; χάρις·33.350 (11) checkable alone
         else lemma               a sense too rare to judge falls back to its lemma, if that is checkable
note     once per lemma           "where ULB says grace and thanks, you write neema for both" (its own channel)
compare  only by a list           { χάρις·88.66, חֵן·… }  side by side, never merged, never a finding
```

- **Alignment trains on lemmas.** Splitting EM by sense would starve it: the NT's 4,824
  content lemmas become 5,869 Louw-Nida senses, and χάρις·thanks has 11 tokens
  (`~/projects/seedwords/LOG.md`, 2026-09-30).
- **The check judges senses.** χάρις has 155 tokens: kindness 88.66 (89), gift 57.103 (19),
  favor 25.89 (14), thanks 33.350 (11). Judged as one lemma, its best rendering covers at most
  57%, under `min_agree` 0.85, so today χάρις is silently never checked. MACULA carries the
  sense: `ln` for Greek, `lexdomain` (SDBH) for Hebrew.
- **A verse is a finding only if it misses at every level that can be judged.** A translator
  who merges senses passes, because each sense and the lemma are consistent. One who splits
  passes too, because each sense is consistent. The finding is a verse that matches no usual
  rendering of its sense, or of its lemma when the sense is too rare to judge.
- **A domain is too coarse.** LN 88 holds ideas a translator rightly renders differently. The
  unit is the sense (88.66), not the domain (88).
- **Hebrew grace isn't Greek grace.** חֵן, חֶסֶד and χάρις are different lemmas, and the LXX mostly
  renders חֶסֶד as ἔλεος. Nothing groups them except an explicit list, and a list's members are
  shown side by side.

## The gateway-only path

Used when MACULA can't ship. The gateway text is registered as a reference corpus
(`updateReference(id, text, { keepText: true })`) and is the source itself. Will: "This is
real and useful linguistically for bible purposes, but I can't force ops […] the fact that the
original open source texts come pre-lemmatized is ideal imo." So the gateway-only path is the
fallback, and the pivot is the case to make (see the `sell-macula` follow-up).

- **Checked by word, and it says so.** No language-specific stemmers (Will). Fragmentation is
  real here: *believe* and *believed* are two terms with smaller populations.
- **Gateway-side letter-window stems: not now.** It would run the stem finder the other way
  round, finding *believ* from the data, but there is no lemma to anchor it. So the target
  would anchor the gateway stems, then the gateway stems would anchor the target stems, which
  is two more EM passes on top of the model and a feedback loop between them. It's a lot of
  cost and risk for the fallback path. A cheaper experiment, if the fallback ever needs it:
  merge gateway forms that the target renders with the same family (*believe*, *believes* →
  both ~*amini*). That uses only links that already exist. Its risk is merging different words
  that one target word happens to render (*faith* and *belief*), which is close to what a sense
  is anyway.
- **No content flag from a Zipf cut.** Measured on en_ulb (66 books), the most frequent words
  include the key terms: *yahweh* is 18th, *god* 34th, *israel* 50th, *king* 52nd and *son*
  53rd. Cutting the most frequent types would delete exactly the terms we want to check. So on
  the gateway-only path, every word is `content`, and two things that already exist do the
  filtering:
  - The check's own floor. A function word that the target renders inconsistently or drops
    (*the* in a language without articles) never reaches `min_agree` 0.85, so it is never
    checkable. That uses the data, in any language.
  - The host's filter on `terms()`. A function word that *is* rendered consistently
    (*and* → *na*) becomes a checkable term, and the host leaves it out of its panel, or the
    findings sort ranks it below the terms that matter.
- **If that proves noisy, use a learned signal, not a list:** a word whose links mostly go to
  NULL or scatter across many target words is acting as a function word. Model 2 already
  computes that, in any language. Measure before building it.

### How phrases come out of word alignments

Yes, it is possible, and it's the standard method. Phrase-based machine translation (Koehn,
Och and Marcu, 2003) gets every phrase from a word alignment in exactly this way, with IBM
models underneath. IBM Model 2 itself only links word to word. A phrase is read off the links
afterwards, with one rule.

**The consistency rule.** A source span and a target span form a phrase pair when:

1. at least one link joins them, and
2. no word inside either span links to a word outside the other span.

```text
source   υἱὸς  τοῦ  ἀνθρώπου          (Son of Man: 5207, 3588, 444)
links    υἱὸς → mwana    ἀνθρώπου → Adamu    τοῦ → NULL
target   … ni  mwana  wa  Adamu  …
span     first..last linked target word = "mwana wa Adamu"
check    does "wa", inside the span, link to anything outside υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου?  no → a clean phrase
```

If "wa" had linked to a Greek word elsewhere in the verse, the pair would fail the rule, and
that verse's rendering would not count. A failure isn't a finding, just a verse where the
phrase isn't cleanly separable (word order moved something into the middle).

**Which source phrases to try** (the "stock phrase" half). There are three ways, from
cheapest to best:

- **Recurring adjacent lemmas.** Count lemma n-grams (2 to 4 words, skipping articles) across
  the testament, and keep those that occur together far more often than chance. That's an
  association score such as log-likelihood or PMI. υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου, βασιλεία τοῦ θεοῦ
  and πνεῦμα ἅγιον all score high. It is computed once from MACULA and goes in the lemma pack,
  so there's no per-project cost.
- **MACULA's syntax trees.** A noun-phrase constituent is a phrase boundary the treebank has
  already decided. It's more accurate, and it's the first real use for a `syntax` pack.
- **A curated list** (key terms, Translation Words). It's the most trusted, and it matches
  only what someone thought to list.

**Does "Holy Spirit" just fall out?** Mostly, but the two halves do different jobs:

- **The association score decides *which* source phrases exist.** It runs on MACULA alone, so it
  is the same for every project and is computed once. Adjacent content lemmas that occur
  together far more than chance are exactly the stock phrases: πνεῦμα ἅγιον (Holy Spirit),
  υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου (Son of Man), βασιλεία τοῦ θεοῦ (kingdom of God), ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν (truly I
  tell you), and in Hebrew יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת (Yahweh of hosts) and כֹּה אָמַר יְהוָה (thus says
  Yahweh). Expect noise too, such as frequent pairs that aren't terms, so the candidate list is
  something to measure and prune, not something to trust blind.
- **The consistency rule decides *how this translation* renders each one**, per verse, from the
  project's links. That's the per-project part.
- **Compounds fall out for free.** If a target language writes Holy Spirit as one word, both
  Greek lemmas link to that one word, the span is one word long, and the rule passes. Hebrew's
  prefixes (*ו*, *ה*, *ל*) are separate words in MACULA, so they behave like Greek articles.
- **What does not fall out:** a stock phrase the translator introduces with no source phrase
  behind it, such as an idiom for one Greek word. That's one lemma with a multi-word rendering.
  One-to-many links handle it as an ordinary lemma term whose rendering happens to be several
  words.

**What a phrase term then is**: a set of lemma positions within a verse, checked like a lemma.
Its renderings are the target spans the rule accepts. Each rendering is the span's word
sequence (or its stems), counted across verses. Usual rendering, `min_agree`, `NearMiss` and
`MissingRendering` all work unchanged. The phrase is a new kind of term, not a new check.

**What can go wrong:**

- **Discontinuous renderings.** "roho … mtakatifu" with another word between them stays a
  phrase only if the intruder links inside the phrase. Otherwise the verse is skipped, and
  skipping is counted, never flagged.
- **Weak links.** The span is only as good as its two end links, which is why the links need
  to be one-to-many (next question).
- **Overlap.** "Holy Spirit" and "Spirit" both count the same πνεῦμα. A finding names the
  longest term that judged it, so one omission is one finding.

The earlier draft held one ~9 MB buffer per testament in JS and patched it with
verse-level deltas. That breaks two ways:

- **Offsets shift.** Adding five bytes to MRK 2:17 moves every later span in the book.
- **Verses shear.** Deleting a `\v` merges two verses, renumbering moves every later key,
  and pasting brings in duplicates. A verse-level delta has to insert, remove and replace
  rows, and an overlay in JS has to get every case right.

**Will's fix is the right one, and it makes hot alignment safe with no delta protocol at all.**
The trick is to make every piece of the buffer content-addressed by its chapter, which is
what galley already does: pantry's chapter tables and aggregates are keyed by `RawChecksum`
and "book-index free", and `wasm.md` says "a chapter that only MOVED keeps its checksum".

### Chapter blocks, keyed by checksum

```text
ALGN   one layout, declared against ticket; a buffer is a run of chapter blocks, plus an optional summary

block      per chapter    book, chapter, RawChecksum (xxh3), model generation u32, byte length u32
  verses   per verse      org key u32, occurrence u8, flags (bridge, duplicate, one side missing),
                          start u32 (relative to the CHAPTER), first source word, first target word
  target   per word       start u16, end u16, relative to its verse
  source   per word       term u32 (lemma·sense, or gateway word), first link, link count
  links    per link       target word u16 (in verse), conf u16      one-to-many (phrase-links)

summary    per testament  check generation u32
  terms    per term       label, lemma, sense, verses u32, carried u32, first form, form count, note
  forms    per form       string, count
  findings per finding    code u8, (checksum, verse, source word) → term, target word | none,
                          usual form, carried u32, of u32, score
  notes    per lemma      lemma, senses (term range), rendering per sense
strings    UTF-8 pool
```

- **Nothing in a block knows where the chapter sits in the book.** Every offset is relative to
  its chapter (verse start) or its verse (word span). The absolute offset is prefix
  arithmetic: the chapter's start in the book, plus the verse's start, plus the word's. The
  chapter starts come from the book's chapter lengths, which galley's Toc already has. That's
  about 1,189 additions for a whole Bible, redone whenever any length changes.
- **A block is valid exactly when two things match**: its `RawChecksum` equals the chapter's
  current checksum, and its model generation equals the current one. The model generation
  changes only on a rebuild (retraining), never on `Tables::update`. An update relinks only the
  verses it was given, and every other verse keeps its stored links, so an untouched chapter's
  block is exactly what a fresh emit would produce.
- **So JS caches blocks by (checksum, model generation), and there are no deltas.** An edit
  changes one chapter's checksum, and that block alone is re-asked. Every other block stays
  valid even though its absolute offsets moved, because it never stored them. Shear,
  renumbering and duplicates are all inside one chapter's block, rebuilt whole, so the hard
  cases never reach JS. An edit that moves a `\c` changes two checksums and re-asks two blocks.
- **The summary is not cached by checksum.** The check reruns whole on every edit (~56 ms), so
  terms, findings and notes can move anywhere. They come in their own small section (tens of
  kB) with the check generation, and are re-asked whenever it moves. A finding names its
  chapter by checksum, so it resolves against the same blocks.

```ts
const blocks = new Map();                                    // key: `${checksum}:${modelGen}`
const block = (book, ch) => blocks.get(key(book, ch)) ?? fetchAndKeep(book, ch);   // galley.alignment(book, { chapter })
a.verse("MRK 2:17")                 // every copy, each with its occurrence; spans absolute u32..u32
a.summary()                         // terms(), findings({ minOf, minAgree }), notes(): re-asked on check generation
```

**For Sefer's infinite scroll**, the view asks for blocks as chapters scroll into range and
keeps them. A whole Bible fully scrolled is the ~9 MB, held as ~1,189 independent blocks rather
than one buffer, and an edit costs one block plus the summary. That's hot alignment without
an overlay, because the only thing that's ever "patched" is a map entry replaced whole.

<Tradeoff id="one-buffer" chose="one layout of chapter blocks keyed by (RawChecksum, model generation), cached by JS, plus a summary section re-asked per check generation" cost="the reader does prefix arithmetic for absolute offsets, and a view must track two generations">
Maintenance: there's no delta protocol and no overlay. Galley emits whole blocks from resident
`Tables`, as it would emit anything else, and the cache rule fits in one line. Flexibility:
the same block serves an editor chapter, an infinite scroll, and the concordance (blocks for
the chapters a term occurs in). Going cold is the degenerate case: a host that caches nothing
just asks every time and is still correct. What stays hard is a rebuild (retrain), which
invalidates every block at once. A host should treat that as a background refresh, not as
part of the edit loop.
</Tradeoff>

### Duplicate verses

Will: "using 'MRK 2:17', not sure our precedent for handling any dupes" … "On duplicates,
what would you do?"

The precedent: `sous_core::alignment::align` pairs duplicates in producer order when both sides
have the same number of them. When the counts differ, it emits
`AlignmentFact::AmbiguousDuplicate` and pairs neither. `GLOSSARY.md` defines the
**occurrence**, the ordinal that tells two `\v 17` apart, as domain-real. MACULA never has
duplicates, so on the pivot path every target duplicate is an unequal count.

What I would do:

1. **Pairing stays as sous does it.** An ambiguous duplicate never enters `Tables`, so it can't
   teach the model or skew a habit. Findings never depend on a guess.
2. **Every copy is scored, not paired.** The model is trained on everything else, so linking a
   copy against a candidate source verse costs microseconds. Score each copy against the source
   verse with its key, and against the source verses within two of it in the same chapter.
3. **Report what the scores support, in three cases:**

```text
one copy clearly matches its key, another matches a neighbour
  → DuplicateVerse  "the second MRK 2:17 aligns with 2:18 (0.79), not 2:17 (0.11)"
     stronger still when the target has no 2:18: "…and MRK 2:18 is missing" (sous's Presence fact)
one copy matches its key, the other matches nothing well
  → DuplicateVerse  "the second MRK 2:17 aligns with no nearby verse (best 0.14)"
neither copy is clearly better (margin under ~0.2)
  → the AmbiguousDuplicate fact only; no finding
```

4. **Every copy is addressable.** Verse rows carry their occurrence, `a.verse("MRK 2:17")`
   returns every copy, and a finding names its copy by occurrence.
5. **Duplicates only in v1.** Scoring every unique verse against its neighbours would also catch a
   mistyped number with no duplicate (2:18 written 2:19), but every verse becomes a candidate
   and it's noisier. That's a later step, after measuring how often `DuplicateVerse` is right.

## Words: one rule, split in Rust, never in JS

Will: "Should probbly uax 29 dep, but sous does hav ea faster path for that […] BUT Idk how
you ensure tht JS can do the same word splitting?"

**JS never splits words.** Every target word's span is in the buffer's `target` section, so a
highlighter reads word boundaries from what Rust split. There's nothing for JS to reproduce,
and the same rule as everywhere else applies: a consumer never re-derives a layout.

Two word rules exist today, and pairing should use one of them:

- `pairing/src/tokens.rs`: letters, marks and digits, joined across one apostrophe or hyphen.
- `sous_core::words` (`sous-chef/core/src/words.md`): letters and glue, extended through one
  nonletter with a letter on both sides. It uses no UAX #29 at runtime, and it is compared
  against `unicode_segmentation` over the 1,504-corpus fleet, with the disagreements recorded
  per script in `sous-chef/evidence.md`. That comparison is the reason not to add a UAX #29
  runtime dependency.

**Rust never fetches.** Galley takes bytes, and where they come from is the host's choice: R2,
bundled in the app for offline users, or cached in IndexedDB. Data is split into **packs**,
one per layer, all keyed the same way: the MACULA word (org verse and word position). A new
layer is a new pack, and nothing already shipped changes.

### How many packs v1 needs: two

Will: "How many of these packs would we actually need at this point btw?"

| Pack | v1? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `lemmas.nt`, `lemmas.ot` | **yes, 2** | The pivot's key: lemma, sense and content flag per word, plus MACULA's English gloss and Strong's in its string table, and the phrase candidates. An NT-only project needs just one. |
| `gateway.*` | no | Computed on the device from the gateway text the host already has, and cached. A prebuilt one is an optional shortcut later. |
| `glosses.*` | no | Labels come from the gateway's own words, with MACULA's English gloss as the fallback. Gloss packs only matter when the UI language isn't English and the gateway link is weak. |
| `syntax.*` | no | Only for tree-based phrase boundaries or the original-languages item. |

So v1 ships two files, about 0.9 MB (NT) and 2.1 MB (OT) with senses (estimated: today's 658 kB NT file plus 2 bytes per word), and the pack
table above is the room to grow.

### What a pack actually is

Will: "lemmas is derived from the macula syntax tree? Stored compact as, json? array
buffer? what actually is it?"

**MACULA** is Clear Bible's annotated Greek NT (SBLGNT) and Hebrew OT (WLC). It ships two
forms of the same data: syntax trees as XML, and a flat TSV export with one row per word
carrying its id, reference, surface text, lemma, Strong's, morphology, English gloss and
sense or domain codes. The worktree's `build_pairing_data` reads **the TSV, not the trees**:
`strong`, `english` and `ln` for Greek, and `strongnumberx`, `english` and `lexdomain` for
Hebrew (`pairing-data/src/bin/build_pairing_data.rs:219`).

**A pack is a flat little-endian binary file, not JSON.** It's an 8-byte magic and version,
then fixed-width arrays, read in place with no parsing step. On the JS side it is just an
`ArrayBuffer` passed through to galley. The lemma pack today (`PSRC0001`,
`pairing-data/src/source.rs`) is:

```text
b"PSRC0001"  u32 lemmas  u32 verses  u32 words  u32 string bytes
u32 × (lemmas + 1)     string offsets; lemma i is "strong\tgloss\tlemma"
u8  × string bytes
u32 × verses           verse ids (org), ascending
u32 × (verses + 1)     first word of each verse
(u16 lemma, u8 flags) × words      bit 0: content word
```

That's 658 kB for the NT. Adding a `u16` sense per word makes it a `PSRC0002`. The other packs
follow the same pattern:

- A **gloss pack** is a string table: one gloss per lemma·sense, in one language.
- A **gateway pack** is one small row per MACULA word: the gateway word index it links to,
  with confidence.
- A **syntax pack** would be parent pointers per word.

All of them use the same word order, so row *n* of every pack is the same Greek or Hebrew
word.

### Loading, step by step

<Calls id="load" title="a host loads the NT lemma pack">
```text
host: bytes = cache.get("lemmas.nt") ?? await fetch(url)   -- the host's choice: R2, app bundle, IndexedDB
  galley.loadPack(bytes)                                     -- copies into wasm memory once
    read magic + version                                     -- unknown or old → throws naming both
    pantry: keep it pinned, count its bytes in budget.rs     -- like any resident product
  galley.pairing… doors now have a source                    -- before this, they throw: no lemma pack loaded
```
</Calls>

For an offline user the host bundles the files in the app, or caches them on first use, and
the sequence is the same with no network. Galley never knows the difference.

| Pack | Per | Holds | From | License |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lemmas.{nt,ot}` | testament | lemma, sense, content flag per word | MACULA (`ln`, `lexdomain`) | CC BY 4.0 |
| `glosses.{nt,ot}.{en,fr,es,zh}` | testament × UI language | a gloss per lemma·sense | UBS dictionaries (SDGNT, SDBH) | CC BY-SA 4.0 |
| `gateway.{en_ulb,…}` | gateway text | the gateway word(s) linked to each MACULA word | our offline Model 2 run | the gateway text's license |
| `syntax.{nt,ot}` (later) | testament | MACULA syntax trees | MACULA | CC BY 4.0 |

```js
galley.loadPack(await host.pack("lemmas.nt"));       // one door for every pack; the magic says which
galley.loadPack(await host.pack("gateway.en_ulb"));
galley.loadPack(await host.pack("glosses.nt.es"));   // optional; labels fall back to the gateway, then MACULA's gloss
```

- The UBS dictionaries carry glosses in English, French, Spanish and Chinese (traditional and
  simplified), with senses and their Scripture references. That's exactly the localization
  split, which is why glosses are a pack per UI language.
- **Share-alike:** the UBS dictionaries are CC BY-SA 4.0. A gloss pack derived from them
  is probably share-alike too, which is one more reason to keep it a separate asset and out of
  wasm and out of `mise`.
- Sizes are estimated. `lemmas` gains a `u16` sense per word over today's file (~+0.9 MB
  across both testaments, which Will said is fine). A gateway pack is about a word index per
  MACULA word (~1–2 MB for a whole Bible).

## Proposed design

What the sections add up to, for Will to accept or change.

1. **Train on lemmas, one model per testament** (agreed).
2. **Pivot by default.** Check target↔MACULA, and label every term with the gateway's own
   words through a gateway alignment computed on the device in a worker (or fetched prebuilt). Fall back to the gloss pack, then MACULA's
   English gloss.
3. **Judge by sense, else by lemma. Merging always passes.** Sense notes are their own channel.
4. **Gateway-only** when MACULA can't ship: checked by word, with no stemmers, no Zipf cut, and
   every word content.
5. **Phrases after alignment**, by the consistency rule over one-to-many links. Candidates come
   from recurring adjacent lemmas scored in the lemma pack. The model is unchanged.
6. **One `ALGN` layout of chapter blocks**, with chapter-relative offsets (absolute offsets by
   prefix arithmetic) and each block keyed by (`RawChecksum`, model generation), plus a summary
   section keyed by check generation. Ship cold first; the JS cache is a later addition with no
   deltas. Duplicate verses are scored, not paired (`DuplicateVerse`).
7. **Words split once, in galley, with `sous_core::words::for_each_word`.** JS reads
   `start..end` spans and never splits.
8. **Data as packs keyed by MACULA word**, supplied by the host, with no network in Rust.
   v1 needs two: `lemmas.nt` and `lemmas.ot`.
9. **Versification is its own layer**, before any pairing
   ([its item](/items/2026-10-14-a-versification-layer-for-everything-that-pairs-ve)).
10. **Measure before any UI.** On the Swahili NT: the checkable gain from senses, the catch
    rate on planted errors, and pivot against gateway-only.
