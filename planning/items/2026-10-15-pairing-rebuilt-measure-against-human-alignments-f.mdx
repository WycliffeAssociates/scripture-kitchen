## What a user gets

A translator reads, in the words of the text they translated from: "Where your source says
*grace*, you write *neema* in 236 of 241 verses. These five verses have none of your usual
words for it." Behind that sentence is a Greek or Hebrew lemma and sense; the user never sees
Greek. Two things change from the old plan:

- **The terms view is the product; findings are a view of it.** For every term and every
  sense, the translator sees the renderings the project uses, with counts, and can mark each
  as approved. That is what Paratext's Biblical Terms tool is, and what consultants already
  trust. "Missing rendering" then means "none of the approved or usual renderings", and the
  check converges to the team's standard instead of to a statistical habit.
- **Names are their own lane.** 4,626 Greek tokens are proper names (MACULA `type=proper`;
  4,691 carry a Louw-Nida 93.* code) and 19,007 Hebrew morphemes sit in SDBH's names domain.
  Names fail differently (transliteration drift, a scanned *I* read as *l*, Elijah beside
  Elias), they are the easiest terms to check, and they are the first thing a checker reads.

## The assumptions the old plan rested on

Each one is either wrong on the raw data, or untested. The stage-0 harness below is what tests
them; the number next to each is from this session, run on the MACULA TSVs in
`~/projects/seedwords/data/raw/`.

|---|---|---|---|

### Measurements, each a ledger row

- **Link quality against gold.** Precision, recall and AER (Och and Ney 2003, sure and
  possible links) on content words, and separately on term words from translationWords. That
  second number is the one the product depends on.
- **Rendering sets against gold.** For each term, the renderings the engine extracts versus
  the renderings a human alignment yields; precision and recall per term, and the share of
  terms reaching checkable.
- **Missing-rendering verdicts.** On the gold-aligned texts, "verse lacks a rendering" has a
  ground truth. Report precision and recall per language, with bootstrap intervals.
- **False positives on untouched text**, per 1,000 verses, on finished Bibles. This is the
  number a translator feels.
- **Catch rate on real errors** from the drafts, and on planted errors of three kinds:
  omission, substitution by another word of the same text, and orthographic near-miss. Planted
  tests stay, but as the cheapest row, not the only one.
- **Buckets, not averages.** Every number by script (Latin, Indic, Arabic, Cyrillic, Ethiopic,
  spaceless) and by WALS 26A and 81A, the way the Model 2 comparison was already reported by
  word order. The worst bucket is the headline.
- **Cost rows**: build time and peak heap per whole Bible, single-thread, native and wasm.

## Stage 1: decisions the harness makes

Each decision below was fixed in the old plan by judgment. Here each is a question with the
experiment that answers it, run on the stage-0 material. The recommendation is what to do if
the numbers are close.

<Question id="direction">Align the target to Greek lemmas, to the gateway's words, or to the gateway's words relabelled with the MACULA lemma each links to (the gateway pack projected onto the gateway text)? The third keeps the gateway's order and phrasing, which the target follows, and still trains on lemma ids, so *believe*, *believes*, *believed* stay one term. Measure all three on link quality, checkable terms and false positives. Recommendation if close: the third, because labels then come from the words that were actually aligned.</Question>

<Question id="aligner">Model 2 in the fast_align form against an HMM aligner (Vogel 1996; dependency-free, a few hundred lines) and against eflomal as the ceiling. Decide by AER on term words and by false positives, per bucket. Recommendation: keep Model 2 if it is within 3 AER points of eflomal on term words in every bucket; otherwise add the HMM.</Question>

<Question id="links">Today a link is the argmax of forward × reverse posterior, one target word per source word. Phrases and one-to-many renderings need more. Compare that against a posterior threshold (≥ 0.2, at most 4) and against grow-diag-final-and, on gold precision and recall of the link *sets*. Recommendation: threshold, since the confidence stays per link and the charter wants numbers a user can read.</Question>

<Question id="units">For spaceless scripts and for type/token ratio above 0.25, stems against a learned BPE against abstention. Use the Chinese and Burmese gold from Clear. Recommendation: whatever the gold says, and a stated abstention like sous's ("no word lane may claim anything in them") where neither works. Not "no BPE" by decree.</Question>

<Question id="thresholds">`min_agree` 0.85, `min_conf` 0.7, `min_form_share` 0.10 were set by eye. Refit them on the gold-derived precision curve and on the drafts, per bucket, and record the curve so a host's sensitivity control means something.</Question>

<Question id="gateway-filter">Expect a rendering in the target only where the gateway rendered the word (the gateway pack has a link for it). ## Stage 2: engine changes that follow from the raw data

These do not wait on stage 1; they are wrong today whatever the aligner.

### Sense sets, and the Hebrew key

A word carries a *set* of senses. The lemma pack stores, per word, a `u16` index into a table
of sense tuples (few distinct combinations exist), not one `u16` sense. The check judges a
multi-tagged token as belonging to each of its senses: it passes if any sense's renderings
cover it. Statistics per sense ("χάρις·88.66, 89 tokens") count single-tagged tokens and say
how many were ambiguous.

The Hebrew key is `strongnumberx` plus `sdbh`. `lexdomain` is never a key. Where `sdbh` is
blank (17% of content morphemes), the key is the lemma alone, which is the fallback the old plan
already had. The pack's string table carries both the LN gloss and the SDBH entry label so a
sense note can name the sense in words.

<Tradeoff id="sense-granularity" chose="LN senses for Greek, SDBH senses for Hebrew, never compared across testaments" cost="the two inventories split differently: LN gives χάρις four senses, SDBH gives חֶסֶד one, so Hebrew sense notes will be rarer and coarser">
This is the data as it exists. A per-testament note says which inventory judged it.
</Tradeoff>

### Names as a lane

Terms whose MACULA type is `proper` (Greek) or whose SDBH domain is names (Hebrew) are a
separate term kind with its own rules: renderings compared as transliterations (near-miss
first, with edit distance scaled by length), a single expected spelling per referent, and the
Greek and Hebrew forms of one person grouped through MACULA's `referent` and
`participantref` columns where they resolve. Findings sort first, because a checker reads names
first and the fix is mechanical.

### Approved renderings as input

`CheckOpts` gains per-term approved renderings: plain forms and Paratext-style wildcards
(`amini*`, `*amini*`). The host persists them; galley passes them; the check judges against
approved ∪ learned, and reports learned-only renderings as suggestions. Suppression stays the
UI's job; approval is the engine's input, because it changes the denominator.

### Disputed words

`build_pairing_data` reads RP2018 beside SBLGNT and flags every SBLGNT word whose lemma is
absent from the Byzantine verse. A finding on a flagged word carries the flag and sorts last;
a host may hide them. Whole verses absent from the critical text (ACT 8:37, 1JN 5:7–8,
JHN 7:53–8:11, MRK 16:9–20 and the like) are the versification layer's and the Presence fact's
business, not pairing's.

<Followup id="disputed-count">Count how many verses and how many term tokens the flag touches. If it is under a few hundred tokens the flag is cheap; if it is thousands, measure the false-positive share it removes on Reina-Valera 1909 (in the committed tier) and Louis Segond 1910.</Followup>

### Determinism, stated honestly

Equality between a fetched pack and a computed one is defined on links: the argmax and a
confidence quantized to `u16`. Floats are never compared. If bit-identity is wanted, the hot loop
uses its own `exp` (a short polynomial, as fast_align effectively does) and a test runs the same
build natively and under wasm and compares bytes. Until that test exists the plan says
"equal on links", not "provably identical".

### The convergence test

Replace the block-cache property test with the test sous already requires of every rule: after
any random edit sequence, incremental `Tables` and a cold rebuild agree on the checkable set
and on findings, and the block for an untouched chapter, emitted from the rebuilt tables, equals
the cached one. Where they diverge, `edits_since_build` is the policy that triggers a rebuild in
the background, and the test says how far drift goes before it matters.

### Carried engine work

Phrases by the consistency rule, with candidates first from translationWords' multi-word entries
(a gold list to measure against), then recurring adjacent lemmas, then syntax constituents.
Duplicate verses scored, not paired (`DuplicateVerse`). One-to-many links. Sense notes on their
own channel.

## Stage 3: packs, seam, wire

Carried from the old plan with these changes:

- **`lemmas.{nt,ot}` v2**: sense-set index, name flag, disputed flag, SDBH labels. Still
  opaque bytes only Rust reads.
- **`terms.tw`**: translationWords keyed by Strong's, CC BY-SA, a separate asset like the UBS
  dictionaries. It ranks findings and seeds the terms view. A host without it still works; it
  just ranks by frequency.
- **`gateway.*`**: where the team's gateway edition is one Door43 has human-aligned, the pack is
  the human alignment. Otherwise it is Model 2, computed on device, and the ledger says how
  far Model 2 is from the human one on the editions where both exist.
- **The project records its gateway's checksum.** A label is only true for the text the
  translator actually read.
- **`ALGN`** chapter blocks keyed by (`RawChecksum`, model generation), summary by check
  generation, cold first: unchanged. The reader exposes the terms view (per term, per sense,
  renderings with counts and approval state) as the first-class read; findings are a filter
  over it.
- Galley splits words with `sous_core::words::for_each_word`; JS never splits; versification
  is its own layer. Unchanged.

<Question id="tw-license">translationWords is CC BY-SA 4.0 and carries a trademark condition. As a separate asset, like the UBS dictionaries, does that fit? If not, the terms pack is an optional host-supplied list in the same format.</Question>

## Out of scope

Similar verses (delete, don't migrate). The parallel-passage check. Original languages in the UI
(its item). ULT in the product: assume no ULT, but ULT's and the GLTs' human alignments are
evaluation data, which the old plan never used.

## Order of work

1. **Stage 0 in full**, nothing else first: loaders, ledger, the fleet buckets, baselines run in
   seedwords. The first ledger rows are today's engine, unchanged, so every later row has a
   "before".
2. **Stage 1's questions answered by rows**, in this order: direction, units, aligner, links,
   gateway filter, thresholds. Each answer is a `<Question answer>` here pointing at its row.
3. **Stage 2**: sense sets and the Hebrew key, names lane, approved renderings, disputed flag,
   determinism rule, convergence test, then phrases.
4. **Migrate trimmed**, as the old plan's step 1, now carrying the harness with it.
5. **Stage 3**: packs, seam, `ALGN`, settings and messages.
6. **The precision study on a live draft** is the gate before any UI: a consultant judges a
   blind sample of flags at the default thresholds, and the share they would act on is a ledger
   row with a target agreed in advance.
