The stage-0 harness that [pairing, rebuilt](/items/2026-10-15-pairing-rebuilt-measure-against-human-alignments-f)
asked for was built in `~/projects/pairing-lab` and run from 15 to 20 October. Its results overturn
one assumption of [the 2026-10-14 plan](/items/2026-10-14-pairing-alignment-consistency-checks-ready-to-migrate-2026-1),
that IBM Model 2 is the cold pass. This item is what the evidence says to build instead. The lab
is uncommitted; findings are numbered in its `RESULTS.md`.

## What a user gets

- **Gateway-language projects** get an alignment made once, offline, by a trained neural aligner:
  11.4 AER on the English ULB against eflomal's 26 and Model 2's ~32 (findings 2, 14). Links it is
  99% sure of are 97.6% right (finding 31).
- **Heart-language projects** get the same aligner cold, even untrained on the language, and a
  better one after their first vetted chapter (finding 57: 32 verses recover 76–93% of the gain
  2,000 give, for four GPU minutes).
- **The check finds what teams actually fix.** On six real heart-language drafts with full
  BTT-Writer history, a possible-typo flag on the first complete draft was later corrected by the
  team 17–42% of the time, against 8–10% for a random rare word, with no language model
  (finding 56). Missing renderings flagged with the trained aligner were later filled 2.6–4.9×
  chance.
- Two lists per book: **likely typos and names** (short: 20–160 per NT), and **possible missing
  renderings** (longer, lower priority). The terms view with approved renderings stays the product.

> Wait, so you're saying multiple models though instead of one? But that's a lot of models to keep around is the thing?

One shared model plus a small adapter per language; see "Models" below.

## Three layers

| layer | where | what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| cold pass | server, GPU | the shared aligner (+ the language's adapter) aligns every unvetted verse | on import, and after each retrain |
| fast loop | device, CPU | Model 2 or the lab's HMM fills only the words an edit changed; vetted and untouched cold links stay verbatim | every chapter save |
| retrain | server, GPU | train the language's adapter on its vetted links; re-run the cold pass on unvetted verses | every N vetted verses or nightly |

The lab measured the fast-loop rule: re-deriving a strong cold pass through the HMM loses 4–11
points (finding 33), and a chapter update costs milliseconds (finding 36). The HMM is only as good
as its start (finding 51), so it never replaces cold links; it fills gaps.

## Models

- **The aligner is ours.** BinaryAlign's method (Latouche et al., ACL 2024) on Microsoft's MIT
  mDeBERTa-v3 base; inference and training in Rust on candle, written from
  `docs/word-aligner-spec.md` only (findings 46, 49). GPU f32 matches the PyTorch reference link
  for link; our trainer reproduces it (11.7 against 11.4).
- **One shared base:** currently `d43multi`, Clear's gold in seven languages, Hebrew, and Door43's
  aligned texts in 16 more (finding 50). Precision near 90 everywhere; Door43 gold roughly halved
  error on most new languages, and the IRVs it never saw improved too.
- **A small adapter per language** (LoRA): the base stays frozen and an adapter is 2–11 MB instead of
  a full copy. It matches or beats a full per-language copy (finding 58), and a stronger shared base
  starts it about a point better.
- Retraining the shared base: every few months, with all gold, a few GPU-hours.

## The alignment pack

The same shape as the [one-file-per-helps-resource proposal](https://claude.ai/artifact/8eUJzps7raMMR6Qjcmqorg):
one sorted file per text and model version, one row per verse in BCV order, and a small index built
at release (book → chapter → byte range, plus the file's checksum). A reader in Rust (native and
wasm) and one in TS fetch a verse, a chapter or scattered verses by HTTP Range or from OPFS, without
reading the rest. The cold pass is an immutable release file; the device's vetted links and
fast-loop links are a small overlay keyed by verse hash, so a chapter save never rewrites the pack.

> Mostly the idea of indexing with and codegen to resolve in BCV addressing space I think

So the alignment pack is not its own format: it is one resource kind in a shared BCV-indexed layer
that helps, glosses, alignments and cached findings all use.

- **One address space.** A key is book · chapter · verse in a named versification, optionally
  narrowed to a word: `MAT 1:3 w4` is the fourth word of the verse under `words/1`. Mapping between
  versifications is the [versification layer](/items/2026-10-14-a-versification-layer-for-everything-that-pairs-ve)'s job, not each resource's.
- **One file shape.** Rows sorted by key, plus a release-built index (book → chapter → byte range,
  checksum, schema version).
- **A schema per resource kind,** from which the readers are generated: Rust types and a reader
  (native and wasm) and TS types and a reader, all answering the same queries (a verse, a chapter,
  a range, scattered keys) against a shared conformance folder. Adding a kind is a schema, not new
  reader code.
- For alignments, a row is a verse key, the two word hashes, and the links as word-number pairs
  with status and confidence; word-level lookups ("what is MAT 1:3 w4 linked to") resolve inside
  the verse row.

- **Index:** book → chapter → byte range; file checksum; model version; splitter version.
- **Per verse:** the verse ref; a 16-hex hash of the source words and of the target words (the
  lab's `pairing-align/0` rule: words joined by U+001F, SHA-256); links as source index, target
  index, status (`v` vetted, `p` proposed, `x` vetted none), optional confidence.
- **Word positions, not byte offsets.** Words come from one shared deterministic splitter (UAX #29,
  case-folded, as the lab uses), re-derived from onion's verse text, so offsets are never stored.
- Size: a whole-NT alignment with confidences is 2.3 MB as text (193k links); about 0.8 MB packed
  before compression.

## The crate: consumable from day one, with or without an editor

> So not only Sefer, but anyone that can show words … So you don't have to have an editor. You can have the same tokenzier + the alignment files.

One crate owns the alignment file format, its schema and validator, and the readers; TS types and
bindings are generated from it, as the workspace already generates onion's. It depends on `mise`
for `words/1` and book keys, and on nothing kitchen-specific, so a reader, a website or another
editor can use it without Sefer.

The surface, roughly:

```text
tokenize(text)            -> word spans of a verse or chapter under words/1
align(text_a, text_b)     -> links, from the fast statistical model (the cold pass arrives as a file)
open(pack, index)         -> a lazy reader over a pack: nothing read until asked
read(address)             -> links for MAT 1:3, MAT 1, or MAT 1:3 w4, by BCV address
validate(pack)            -> schema, index checksum, words version, hashes
```

**Spans for the DOM.** `tokenize` returns where to slice, not strings: per verse, a flat
`Uint16Array` of pairs (gap since the previous word's end, word length) in **UTF-16 code units**,
so a page lays out words as spans with `text.slice()` and never re-implements the splitter. Rust
works in bytes; the conversion is `mise`'s `Utf16Table`. Per verse, u16 is ample (a verse is far
under 65,535 code units); a chapter-level call returns one array per verse.

**Binary on disk.** It is nearly all small numbers, so the pack is binary: per verse, the two
16-hex word hashes, then links as varint word indices with a status nibble and a confidence byte
(quantised, 0–255); a release-built index (book, chapter, byte range, checksum, words version,
model version) in front. About 0.8 MB for an NT before compression, against 2.3 MB as text. A
JSON or TSV dump of any range is a debug and interchange view, not the format.

**Spike result (lab finding 59, `~/projects/pairing-lab/alignpack/`).** The ULB head alignment
packs to 724 KB (423 KB gzipped) against 2.3 MB of text; all 193,349 links round-trip, confidence
within one quantisation step. A remote reader opens with a 1.3 KB head and reads a chapter in about
2–3 KB (`viewer/pack.html` over HTTP Range: 3.3 KB for MAT 1). Reads take microseconds. wasm is
110 KB (47 KB gzipped), of which UAX #29 and `to_lowercase` are 37 KB (15.5 KB gzipped): the size
argument for `words/1`. Decisions the spike made, to keep:

- a fixed 64-byte header carries the index length, so a remote open is exactly two fetches; the
  index and the body each carry a checksum;
- two words-version tags, source and target (the source hash is over the original-language
  surface words, not the target splitter);
- confidence is one byte, `round(p × 255)`, so thresholds are stated as codes (0.99 → `q ≥ 253`);
  absence is a flag bit;
- the chapter is the smallest fetch (no per-verse offsets in the index: they would add ~16 KB to
  save a few KB per read);
- `wN` in an address is the target word, counted from 1; links are stored 0-based;
- vetted-none (`x`) is a link with no target, in the same sorted list;
- rows do not repeat book or chapter; the index supplies them;
- async sources plan → fetch → decode; the Rust reader stays synchronous with no network code.

### Writing back

> we will need a way to write back and regen the index somehow … That viewer will need to become a real boy, but really it should be composable from the primitives in that lab you wrote.

The pack is immutable; edits live in an **overlay** keyed by verse and by the verse's word hashes,
and a **compaction** step turns pack + overlay into a new pack with a regenerated index.

- **Overlay:** per verse, the edited links (vetted, vetted-none, removed) and the hashes of the
  words they were made against. It is what the editor saves (today's `pairing-align/0` export
  maps onto it) and what a device syncs.
- **Reading:** pack + overlay, overlay winning for a verse whose hashes still match; a verse whose
  words changed shows its overlay links as stale.
- **Compaction:** merge, rewrite rows in BCV order, regenerate the index and checksums, and record
  the parent pack's checksum in the header. A whole NT rewrites in milliseconds, so the index is
  always regenerated, never patched.
- **Chapter-granular update:** `replace_chapter(pack, chapter, rows)`, because Sefer saves whole
  chapters; every other chapter's bytes are unchanged apart from offsets.
- **Where each runs:** the device keeps pack + overlay and may compact locally; the server compacts
  vetted links into the next cold-pass pack, so a retrain never forgets them.

The editor becomes a thin UI over these primitives (`tokenize`, `open`/`read`, overlay, `compact`,
`validate`), so Sefer, a reader app or the lab viewer compose the same pieces.

### Durability

- A link is applied only while both verse hashes match; an edited verse drops its stale links and
  the fast loop refills its changed words.
- Vetted links are never replaced by a retrain.
- "Fetched equals computed" holds **per model version**: the same model and text give the same
  links (exactly in f32, near-exactly in f16). A retrain is a new version and may move unvetted
  links; the UI says so ("aligner updated: 214 links changed in Luke").

| user action | meaning | goes to |

| approve renderings of a term | a team standard (*neema* and *rehema* are both fine for χάρις) | the check: approved renderings never flag |
| dismiss a finding | this verse is fine | a suppression keyed by verse hash + lemma: it returns if the verse changes |
| mark a spelling pair fine | *massih* / *messih* is a variant | a pair-level suppression for near misses and names |

**Heart-language labels are against the gateway, not Greek.** Teams read the gateway; they confirm
"my word matches that ULB word", and the system composes through the gateway's own aligner links
to key the check on Greek and to train the adapter. Composition through a related gateway beat
eflomal by 5–9 points (finding 52) and was confirmed on real drafts (finding 56); through an
unrelated one it loses, so the trained aligner is the default and the composition the CPU-only
fallback.

## The check: changes from the lab's findings v2

Measured on six finished Bibles (findings 53–55) and six real drafts (finding 56):

1. **Near miss gated on frequency:** the candidate occurs at most twice, the usual form at least 3×
   as often. False possible typos 19 → 1.4 per 1,000 verses; *will*/*kill*, *nombre*/*hombre* gone.
2. **Near miss tested before the stem match**, so *jerubalen* no longer hides behind *jeru*:
   misspellings caught 44% → 67%.
3. **Gateway prior:** drop a missing rendering where the gateway also lacks its rendering in that
   verse; halves findings for 1–5 points of deletions. A multi-translation prior trims more.
4. **Names lane:** every spelling aligned to one proper-name lemma; minority spellings flagged.
5. **Drift by book:** a lemma rendered one way in some books and another elsewhere; teams evened
   these out a third of the time in the histories.
6. **On drafts, no affix gate.** The gate that ignores pairs differing only by a common affix was
   tuned on finished Bibles; on drafts the misspellings themselves create such alternations and
   it hid half the reachable typos. Without it, twice as many at the same precision.
7. **Read external links without their probabilities** for rendering counts (finding 53).

Not worth it: Damerau or weighted edit distances, Jaro-Winkler, whole words in place of stems,
Greek senses as the key (they removed variation but made new terms checkable that were also
variation).
