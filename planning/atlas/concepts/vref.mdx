## The problem

Alignment and MT tools want ebible's vref shape, where line N is the same verse in every translation. Bridges (`\v 2-3`) and wrapped, footnoted verses break that if rendered naively.

## The solution

Render the verse-text [[mask]] by [[toc]] rows. A bridge writes its text on its first verse's line and `<range>` on each verse it covers, so later lines do not shift. It does no filtering of its own; what counts as verse text is the mask's recipe. Lossy by name ([[invariants]] 1).

## Trade-offs

- Newlines and tabs inside a verse become spaces.
- Verses the book never had get no blank line; that needs a versification table onion does not own (planning item 2026-10-14).

## Show me

`playground --vref` on the scratch book in [[cst]]. Verses 2-3 are combined, so verse 3 is `<range>`:

```text
MRK 1:1	Jesus wept. Then
MRK 1:2	he Lord   spoke.
MRK 1:3	<range>
MRK 1:4	Bread.
```

## How it works

- **One iterator, many formats.** `verses` is the core; `keys`, `lines` and `joined` are one-liners over it. ebible ships keys and lines as two parallel files keyed by line number.
- **Swap the mask, change the reading.** `Filter::verse_text()` for plain text; the same with notes set to `Unwrap` folds footnote prose in.
- **Trim lives here, not in the mask.** A vref line's edges are presentation; a mask offset must keep naming a real document byte.
- Bridges come from the [[designator]]'s hull.

## Composes with

Reads the [[toc]] and a [[mask]]; bridges come from the [[designator]]'s hull. Used by `playground --vref` for `debug/` dumps.
