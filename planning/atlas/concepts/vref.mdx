## The problem

Many Bible tools, such as alignment and machine translation, want scripture as one verse per line. Line 1 is Genesis 1:1 in every translation, so two files line up just by line number. A USFM file does not look like that: verses wrap, carry footnotes, and sometimes combine, as in "verses 2-3".

## The solution

We take the book's verse-text [[mask]] (markup and footnotes already removed) and its verse index (the [[toc]]), and print one line per verse. A combined verse puts its text on its first verse's line and writes `<range>` on each verse it covers. Dropping those lines would shift every later verse onto the wrong line. This follows ebible's vref format. The renderer does no filtering of its own; what counts as verse text is decided by the mask passed in.

## Trade-offs

- Newlines and tabs inside a verse become spaces, because a line cannot hold a newline or a column a tab.
- Verses the book never had get no blank line. That needs a versification table, which onion does not own (planning item 2026-10-14).

## Show me

`playground --vref` on the scratch book in [[cst]]. Verses 2-3 are combined, so verse 3 is `<range>`:

```text
MRK 1:1	Jesus wept. Then
MRK 1:2	he Lord   spoke.
MRK 1:3	<range>
MRK 1:4	Bread.
```

## How it works

- **One iterator, many formats.** `verses` is the core; `keys`, `lines` and `joined` are one-liners over it. ebible ships keys and lines as two parallel files keyed by line number.
- **Swap the mask, change the reading.** `Filter::verse_text()` for plain text; the same with notes set to `Unwrap` folds footnote prose in.
- **Trim lives here, not in the mask.** A vref line's edges are presentation; a mask offset must keep naming a real document byte.
- Bridges come from the [[designator]]'s hull.
