## The problem

Drafts often arrive as bare `\v` plus text. Teams want them laid out like a source Bible (Gen 2:23 as four poetry lines); doing it by hand is slow, and guessing a mid-verse break position across languages is not reliable.

## The solution

Read the source's layout as breaks addressed by verse ("`\p` before v24", "second `\q` line inside v23"). Verse identity holds across languages, so breaks between verses land exactly; breaks inside a verse are inserted empty after its text for the translator to fill. No proportional guessing ([[invariants]] 3). The output is one set of [[edit]]s offered as a suggestion, never a diagnostic.

## Trade-offs

- Breaks inside a verse arrive empty.
- Footnotes are not copied.
- The source must keep its text in the [[pantry]] (`keepText`).

## Show me

From `galley/src/overlay.md` (GEN 2:21–24). The source's six breaks become six inserts in the target:

```text
skeleton(source)
  GEN 2:21 leading 1 p       GEN 2:23 inside 1 q1     GEN 2:23 inside 2 q2
  GEN 2:23 inside 3 q1       GEN 2:23 inside 4 q2     GEN 2:24 leading 1 p

overlay(target, source) → 6 edits, ascending, non-overlapping
  insert "\p\n"   at the target's \v 21
  insert "\n\q1" "\n\q2" "\n\q1" "\n\q2"   after verse 23's text
  insert "\p\n"   at the target's \v 24
```

## How it works

- **Address = (verse sid, leading | inside, ordinal)**, plus the marker as a check.
- **Empty blocks fold by onion's own [[lint]]** (`Code::EmptyParagraph`), not a second copy of the rule.
- **Invariant:** apply the edits, re-extract, and the target's skeleton equals the source's with empty blocks folded. `tests/overlay.rs` states the comparison itself so it cannot drift into agreement with the library.
- Verses are paired through `sous_core::align` over both sides' [[onion-book]]s.

## Composes with

Reads the [[cst]], [[lint]] report and [[mask]] from the [[pantry]]. Pairs verses through `sous_core::align` over both sides' [[onion-book]]s. Emits [[edit]]s as onion-wasm's `Edits` class.
