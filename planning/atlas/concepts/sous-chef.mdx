## The problem

A translation team proofreading a New Testament wants to catch slips: a comma before a digit where the rest of the book never does that, or a verse far shorter than its neighbours. Many of these languages have no spell-checker, dictionary or grammar model. So the question is: how do you flag likely mistakes in a language no tool has ever seen?

## The solution

Sous Chef learns what this translation usually does by counting, then points at the places that differ. It never says "this is wrong". It says "this happens 12 times out of 9,812", and a person decides. The obvious alternative, a trained language model or dictionary, does not exist for most of these languages, so nothing like that ships. It is a separate product from the USFM engine so it can run on any text source, not only USFM files.

## Trade-offs

- It can only report what is rare, not what is wrong. A rare but correct form still shows up and needs a human to dismiss it.
- It needs enough text to count. On a tiny corpus it abstains rather than guess.
- Signals stay separate. You get several plain reasons, never one blended confidence score.

## How it works

The product promise (`charter.md`):

- **Narrow, truthful claims.** "This form occurs 1 of 1,053 times" is valid; "this is wrong" usually is not.
- **Every verdict shows its numerator, denominator, population and abstention condition.** Unrelated signals are not blended into a confidence number.
- **Patterns first, sites second.** One review row may expand to every matching span.
- **Useful on a new language from the first corpus.** No language model, dictionary or trained artifact ships.
- **Structure comes from Onion.** Sous does not parse USFM; it reads a neutral projection [[galley]] builds.

Measurements behind each rule are in `sous-chef/evidence.md`; checks are defined in `sous-chef/rules/`.
